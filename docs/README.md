# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

## Overview

OctoAcme follows a structured project management approach built around customer value, iterative delivery, clear ownership, and measurable outcomes. The project lifecycle begins with validating a business need and aligning stakeholders, then moves into planning, execution, release, and continuous improvement. This documentation is intended to provide a practical, consistent framework for cross-functional teams working on product features, services, and integrations.

The overarching process emphasizes transparency, accountability, and evidence-driven decisions. Teams are expected to document goals, risks, dependencies, and quality expectations early, while also maintaining a regular communication rhythm to surface blockers and keep stakeholders informed. By standardizing how initiatives are initiated, planned, tracked, and released, OctoAcme reduces ambiguity and helps teams deliver predictable, high-quality outcomes.

## Project Lifecycle

1. Initiation — Validate the problem, confirm stakeholders, define goals and success measures, and decide whether to move forward.
2. Planning — Turn the approved initiative into a prioritized backlog, timeline, milestones, risks, and Definition of Done.
3. Execution — Build, test, review, and iterate in small increments while tracking progress and unresolved issues.
4. Release — Prepare, validate, deploy, and communicate the release with clear rollback and verification steps.
5. Close & Retrospective — Capture lessons learned, document improvements, and feed them back into future work.

## Core Principles

- Customer-first: prioritize usability, value, and stakeholder outcomes.
- Iterative delivery: ship small, testable increments instead of waiting for a big-bang release.
- Clear ownership: each project has a named Project Manager and Product Lead.
- Data-informed decisions: measure results and adapt based on evidence.
- Psychological safety: encourage candid feedback, learning, and continuous improvement.

## Core Roles and Responsibilities

- Project Manager (PM): coordinates schedule, risks, dependencies, and communication.
- Product Manager (PdM): defines outcomes, prioritizes the backlog, and measures success.
- Developers: implement and test features, participate in design, and help identify technical risks.
- QA / Testing: validates quality, acceptance criteria, and regression risk.
- Stakeholders: provide input, approvals, and sponsorship throughout the project lifecycle.

## Team Rhythm and Communication

OctoAcme emphasizes a regular working cadence to keep projects on track. The team uses daily standups to review progress, identify blockers, and clarify dependencies; weekly syncs to assess delivery and raised risks; and demos or milestone reviews to align stakeholders on what has been delivered and what remains. Communication is designed to reduce ambiguity and ensure decisions are made with the right people involved.

In addition to formal meetings, project documentation should remain current and accessible. A single source of truth—such as the project README, project board, or release note—helps align stakeholders, reduce confusion, and ensure critical updates are captured consistently.

## Quality and Delivery Practices

Quality is treated as a continuous responsibility rather than a final checkpoint. The process expects unit tests for new logic, integration tests where relevant, smoke tests for critical user journeys before release, security scanning in CI, and manual QA when needed. Pull requests are expected to be small and reviewable, include issue links and acceptance criteria, and pass automated checks before review approval is granted.

This approach helps teams maintain release quality while still moving quickly. It also supports a healthy delivery rhythm where work is broken into manageable increments, reviewed often, and improved continuously based on both team feedback and observed outcomes.
